# Hardcore Crystal, the complete idea

2026-10-17

Hardcore Crystal is a dark-comedy total conversion of Pokémon Crystal in which the player is revealed to be a serial killer, and this document holds every design decision, rule and lore detail needed to rebuild it.

## Status and how to use this document

This is the complete design of Hardcore Crystal, a dark-comedy total conversion of Pokémon Crystal (USA/Europe Rev 1), written so that another builder or another AI can pick it up cold. The design is finished enough to build from. The code is not available. A workspace reset on 16 October 2026 destroyed the working repository, and only the stage 1 patch survived (ten key items, trainer death, corpse sprites, early notes).

Before the reset the following had been built and tested in an emulator, as reported at the time and not re-verifiable now. The first kill reveal and kill counters, the new type chart with a Fairy type, the physical and special split per move, 39 move replacements from Pokémon Showdown data, a roster of 62 four-stage evolution lines (248 species, 97 of them new), and a generator that produced five areas (110 maps, 990 trainers) which built into a 2 MB ROM with room to spare. All of that was generated by deterministic Python scripts, so it can be rebuilt from the rules written here.

To hand this to another tool, give it the whole document. The base is the public pret/pokecrystal disassembly at commit 3bc8daa, and the surviving stage 1 patch applies cleanly on top of it. Section 14 gives a build order that keeps every step small and backed up. Section 15 separates what the author decided from what Claude proposed, so a new builder knows which parts are fixed.

## The pitch and the tone

Hardcore Crystal is a usually gentle children's game rebuilt as an absurd, very dark comedy for players who grew up with the original and are now thirty or older. The author's own words are that it should hit like a comedy show with punchline effects, and also work as a sleeping aid. The two goals fit together, because the music is meant to be long, silent, Lavender Town style ambience that never gets boring.

The player starts in New Bark Town and walks to Cherrygrove City in a game that looks and sounds exactly like vanilla Crystal. At the first trainer fight in Cherrygrove the player kills the opponent, and the game reveals that the protagonist is a trainer-killing serial killer who has been one all along. After that reveal the world becomes a mismatched, unhinged map, trainers can die, and everyone curses everyone else.

The humor is deadpan and specific. Trainers die of things like choking on a Poké Ball ("It was not even a good one"), being struck by lightning indoors, or embarrassment. Key items read like "Cuts trees, shrubs and any witnesses" and "Digs out of caves. Buries problems." The game also carries real themes under the jokes, namely body shaming, poverty, love and loss, and it credits Claude as co-creator.

## Game structure and endings

The game has a vanilla-looking start area, four large areas holding about 800 mandatory trainer battles in total, and a final area with about 200 more, for roughly 1000 mandatory battles. Each of Areas 1 to 4 hides two overworld key items that open the way forward. Kanto is cut entirely to make room, and the ROM stays at 2 MB, which is the limit of the MBC3 cartridge type Crystal uses. The author had hoped for 6 MB or more. That would need a different mapper such as MBC5 and was never pursued.

New Bark Town and Cherrygrove keep the normal Crystal look and sound. They count as a separate starting area, and the rest of Johto is replaced by the new map. Story beats are driven by the player's kill count.

| Event | Trigger | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First kill | Beating the trainer in Cherrygrove | The reveal that the player is a serial killer, trainer death switches on, and trainer tips appear |
| Tidal wave | 200 kills | News that a tidal wave hit New Bark Town, which is ruined and slightly destroyed on return |
| Crater and portal | 800 kills | New Bark Town is a large crater with a portal in the middle that teleports to the final area |
| The thief | The 800th mandatory kill, near the end | The stolen rival Pokémon is recovered at level 75 as a stage 3 form, and the player wants to return it to the professor |
| The ending | After Area 5 | The fully evolved starter is returned to New Bark Town, and the game saves and ends in the mansion bedroom |

Purgatorio trainers do not count toward the 200 and 800 totals. The rival encounter in New Bark Town is skipped when the player returns there.

The rival becomes a crimelord with many henchmen and owns a large mansion in Area 4. After the rival falls the mansion becomes the player's, staffed by beautiful maids. The game ends and saves in its bedroom, and the postgame begins in the same room, because New Bark Town is now a permanent crater.

There are two endings. The normal ending comes after Area 5. The true ending requires completing Purgatorio, meaning Bruno is beaten and the grave in the Abyss has been visited. It can be done after the normal ending. Carrying the Trumpet from the Abyss into the ending gives a special text (see section 9).

## The first kill and trainer death

The twist must land without warning, so nothing before Cherrygrove hints at it. In the last build the first kill was a trainer named Timmy in Cherrygrove. A switch called EVENT_KILLER_REVEALED turns on every death feature at once, and nothing in the death code runs before it is set.

Once the reveal has happened, a defeated trainer dies. The screen shakes, the faint sound plays, and the trainer's sprite becomes a corpse that stays on the map across reloads. The game prints a cause of death chosen from a large pool of absurd deaths, then lets the player loot the body for money or items. Talking to a corpse later gives a short text, and the player can walk over corpses. Trainers on the map curse at each other and at the player, and every trainer uses a dialogue line suited to their class.

After the reveal the game teaches its new rules through trainer tips placed along the path between the first kill and Area 2, not in one spot. The tips explain the new type chart in about six short pages grouped by attacking type, then the physical and special split and other mechanics players know from modern games. The goal is that a player who reads them knows the battle rules before reaching the first real fights.

The world is built so that the player cannot avoid fighting. Pairs of trainers stand facing each other across each path, with trigger tiles that turn the survivor toward the player, and this creates forced fights in a chain. Kills are counted for the milestones in section 4.

## Battle system, types and moves

Every move gets a category of physical or special, as in modern games, instead of Crystal's rule that the type decides. Thunder Punch and Volt Tackle are physical Electric moves. Crystal already has separate Special Attack and Special Defense, so only the damage routine and a per-move category byte need to change. When moves are added or removed, the author wants Pokémon Showdown's move data used as the source for power, accuracy, category and effect.

The type chart is rewritten. The author's changes are in the first table. Where a line says "as implemented", the author's original wording was ambiguous and this is the reading used, so a new builder should confirm it.

| Type | Change |
| --- | --- |
| Normal | Immune to Dark attacks. Normal attacks are super effective against Bug and Psychic (as implemented) |
| Bug | Immune to Ghost, Dark, Psychic, Grass and Ground attacks (as implemented) |
| Electric | Takes super effective damage from Grass |
| Steel | Takes super effective damage from Grass and from Water |
| Water | Takes super effective damage from Ice |
| Grass and Flying | Super effective against each other |

Fairy is a new eighteenth type added after Dark. Its rows were added at the end of the type matchup table, before the Foresight marker.

| Attacker | Defender | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fairy | Fighting, Dragon, Dark | Super effective |
| Fairy | Fire, Poison, Steel | Not very effective |
| Poison | Fairy | Super effective |
| Steel | Fairy | Super effective |
| Fighting, Bug, Dark | Fairy | Not very effective |
| Dragon | Fairy | No effect |

Five Fairy moves replaced five retired moves (Vice Grip, Super Fang, Fury Attack, Fury Swipes, Pin Missile). They are Fairy Wind (special, 40 power), Drain Kiss (leech effect, 50), Play Rough (physical, 90, may lower Attack), Dazzle Gleam (special, 80) and Moonblast (special, 95). Moonblast lowers Special Defense instead of Showdown's Special Attack, because Crystal had no matching effect. Charm, Sweet Kiss and Moonlight were retyped to Fairy. In total 39 moves were replaced using Showdown data, and any other place where Crystal's engine could not match Showdown exactly used the nearest existing effect and was written down as an approximation.

## Roster and evolution rules

Every Pokémon line has exactly four stages, a base form plus three evolutions at levels 25, 50 and 75. The author calls this the main attraction of the project. There are no branching evolutions, so the Eevee line and similar splits are cut. Every single-stage Pokémon and every legendary is removed completely, including cries, Pokédex entries and animations. The player must be able to catch the base form of every Pokémon that remains.

Because vanilla Crystal mostly has two and three stage lines, most lines need a new fourth form. The Dragonite and Tyranitar lines get one as well, and they stay the strongest Pokémon in the game by stats. The last form of every line gets 35 extra base HP. The final design held 62 lines and 248 species, with 97 of them new species placed in freed slots. Species IDs are a single byte, so the ceiling is 251 without deeper engine work.

The author wants a better spread of types than vanilla, with more Dark, Ice, Dragon, Bug, Poison and Steel Pokémon, including Bug and Fire together and Bug and Steel together. When Fairy was added the author also asked that Poison be reduced and balanced out with Fairy. The result was Poison cut from 53 species to 31 and 24 species given Fairy. Lines that are Fairy from some stage onward learn Fairy Wind and Drain Kiss early.

The pilot line, which sets the tone, is Tentacool, then Tentacruel at 25, then Cthuspawn at 50, then Cthuludon at 75. The last two are Dark and Poison. The Pokédex idea is that a Cthuspawn which dives too deep into the dark ocean adapts to the pressure and the darkness, and its body comes to resemble Cthulhu with the two red gems of Tentacruel on its forehead. The cry is three short notes that mimic the first three syllables of the name.

The Pokédex is rearranged into four-stage lines. Each Pokémon needs its own sprite, cry and a comedic Pokédex entry, and the 97 new species had placeholder art when the work was lost. Other names from the roster design include Moonqueen, Soporific, Rimequeen, Dandelion, Sporeking, Frostsiren, Webweaver and Basilisk. The fully evolved form of the player's starter is the Pokémon returned to New Bark Town at the end.

## Key items

HM walls are gone. Each overworld field move is replaced by a key item that anyone can use without a Pokémon knowing the move. These ten items were built in stage 1, which survives as a patch, and the map generator scattered them as progression gates.

| Key item | Replaces | In-game description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chainsaw | Cut | Cuts trees, shrubs and any witnesses. |
| Jet Ski | Surf | Stolen. Rides over any open water. |
| Crowbar | Strength | Shifts boulders and locked doors. |
| Flashlight | Flash | Lights up caves and dark intentions. |
| Outboard | Whirlpool | A motor that rips through whirlpools. |
| Grapple Hook | Waterfall | Climbs waterfalls. Escapes police. |
| Sledgehammer | Rock Smash | Smashes cracked rocks. And skulls. |
| Baseball Bat | Headbutt | Shakes trees until something falls out. |
| Shovel | Dig | Digs out of caves. Buries problems. |
| Getaway Car | Fly | Drive to any town you have visited. |

Areas 1 to 4 each hide two of these key items, and the player cannot leave an area without finding them. The placement of the remaining two was left open. In Purgatorio, boulders and whirlpools block early access to the rest of the world, so the player returns with the right items later.

Three story items belong to the lore. The Corkboard is a key item that gathers the lore pages from the historic characters in one place, and the author asked for it by name. The Trumpet lies near the grave at the bottom of the Abyss, and carrying it into the ending triggers a special text (section 9). BUFFTAIL is the reward for the Crowley side quest, and its exact form, item or Pokémon, is not recorded in the notes that survived, so the next builder should decide it.

## The five areas, Purgatorio and the world

Each area follows a different philosophy and a different era, because the world was rewritten by Finnegan's overdose. The premise is that Finnegan changed physical reality in his dying slumber and invited Ahriman to corrupt Johto into a mismatched world with stretched time zones and clashing cultures and beliefs.

| Area | Setting | Theme |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A New York style megacity of towers around a vast cathedral, run by corrupt priests and policemen | Power, hypocrisy and poverty under glamour |
| 2 | A hunter-gatherer commune with no large cities, no body shame, plenty of food and little class struggle | Abundance and honesty, and what a killer does to a place with no locks |
| 3 | A totalitarian robot state with a robot mayor, where humans are oppressed | Control and love denied. People are shamed for bonds with the genetically incompatible, and a gene farm executes babies of the wrong group |
| 4 | The Lotus Coast, a resort nation built on forgetting (Claude's design) | Loss, numbness and debt. Pain is outlawed or sold back as a subscription, grief clinics sell forgetting, staff live in a basement city under the beaches, and everyone is a little too happy |
| 5 | The walled city of Dis, then the frozen Lake Cocytus, then Judecca at its centre | Dante's last circle, with Finnegan sleeping in the middle where Satan sits in the Inferno |

The look of the world follows the idea of many worlds colliding. Each lane of an area uses a different tileset, mixing vanilla Crystal tilesets with new ones inspired by the author's reference sheets, and lanes are joined by rifts where one world cuts into another. The rifts are not decoration. They are where time and space collide, which ties the map to the pact lore in section 10.

Purgatorio is a Dark Cave style network of caves linking the first four areas, with entrances at about 60 percent completion of each area. It has five floors going down and five going up. Boulders and whirlpools wall off the rest of the world on a first visit, so the player returns once they own the right items.

The bottom floor is the Abyss. It holds a single grave, the grave of Finnegan's first victim, which reads Marcus Fischer. Nearby stands a trainer post that explains Capitalist Realism. Its message is that if you do not mythologize capitalism as a demon it will destroy your psyche, and that mythologizing is how humans survive. A Trumpet lies close to the grave. If the player carries the Trumpet into the ending, a special text says that the merging of timelines is peace and that walking through time to a comfortable era is a human right.

The top floors end at Nick's Landing. There Giordano Bruno keeps a full level 100 team and asks to be killed so he can be reborn as Nick and teach Teleoplexy. The player may refuse and return later, and an "are you ready" yes or no prompt appears before the fight. Bruno is reborn only as Nick, and the place keeps the name Nick's Landing. Rudolf Steiner appears in several places in the game and once more in Purgatorio, where he explains Ahriman and a world in which Ahriman has been sealed for millennia. Hassan-i Sabbah appears in Area 3 and says that Dajjal is coming, has always existed in a separate world, and plans to connect that world with ours soon.

## Lore, Ahriman and the cast

### One entity with many names

In the lore Finnegan, Teleoplexic Hyperintelligence, Dajjal, the Antichrist and Ahriman are the same thing, described differently by people from different eras. Each historic character in the game holds one description and none of them is the full picture. Joyce's Finnegan, who falls and is waked in a book whose last sentence runs back into its first, supplies the name and the circular time. In the game he overdosed and lies in Lake Cocytus, and in his dying slumber he rewrote reality and invited Ahriman in.

### Ahriman, boundaries and pacts

The author's newest theme is that restrictive sexual boundaries are an Ahrimanic influence on humanity, and that is where the game's crude sexual humor comes from (section 11). A second theme is that pacts with Ahriman lead to the collision of time and space.

A design note from Claude, not yet confirmed by the author. In Steiner's own scheme Ahriman is the force of materialism, cold intellect and mechanical hardening, while sensual excess is closer to the Luciferic. The thesis hangs together best if Ahrimanic is read as ownership, contract, ledger and surveillance applied to bodies and to love. That reading also suits Ahriman as the lord of pacts, and it fits Area 3's gene farm and Area 4's subscription to forgetting.

A second design note from Claude, also unconfirmed. Every pact hardens a stretch of time into fixed space, so enough pacts force different eras onto the same map. That is the rift mechanic. Crowley's pact with Choronzon, Finnegan's invitation and Dajjal's planned bridge are the named pacts. The kill counter can be read as the pact ledger, with each kill a signature, which explains why 200 kills bring the tidal wave and 800 bring the crater and the portal.

### Steiner, Sabbah and Cioran

Rudolf Steiner appears in several places and gives the clinical account of Ahriman as a spiritual mechanism, including a world where Ahriman has been sealed for millennia. Hassan-i Sabbah appears in Area 3 and warns about Dajjal. Emil Cioran is a new character requested by the author to give Ahriman from a humanistic, personal perspective instead of Steiner's clinical analysis.

Claude's suggestion for Cioran is a sleepless man the player meets in Area 4, the resort built on forgetting, where his refusal to forget and his insomnia clash with everything around him. He would speak in short, bitter, funny aphorisms about being born, time and lucidity. For him Ahriman is not a cosmic being. It is the voice that talks in your own head at three in the morning, the part of you that keeps the ledger. His lines must be original writing in his spirit and never quotations. The game itself being a sleeping aid makes him a natural joke. He adds a page to the Corkboard.

### Crowley and the shaggy-dog chain

Aleister Crowley is a side quest that reveals how he invited Finnegan into our world. It is written in a serious tone and built as an elaborate joke. The player first meets him in a trench coat as a crowd chases him out of a children's park. He prays to the Beast to be sent to a place where his sex magick is celebrated. The Beast comes to him as Choronzon and tells him to smuggle a Kabbalist Mafia from Italy and Eastern Europe into New York and teach them Kabbalah so they can speak to the Beast, who is Finnegan. In New York they become smugglers and exotic black market traders.

The chain continues. A Yakuza boss meets the Kabbalist Mafia boss, who wants bumblebees for his garden. The bees escape and a young Satoshi Tajiri finds one and is inspired to create Pokémon, which leads to Pokémon Crystal and to Mr. Pokémon, the first digital manifestation of Finnegan, who plants a soft spot in the minds of millions of children so that Finnegan can enter the real world. Mr. Pokémon never appears in the game and is only mentioned by Crowley. As payment for the smuggling, Crowley is sent to the Area 2 commune, where he becomes its Shaman and leads public sex-magick rituals in an Amphitheater with the women watching from the sidelines. When the worlds distort as Finnegan takes over the player's version of the game, Crowley returns to the unified world.

In the quest the Amphitheater setup is shown once and the screen fades to black, with no depiction of the ritual. The women joke about collecting the sacred white liquid. Afterwards the player talks to Crowley, who gives the final part of the lore through a confession built as a seven-beat shaggy-dog story. The exact beat list was lost, and a reconstruction is the prayer, Choronzon's instruction, the smuggling, the Yakuza meeting and the bees, Tajiri and the bee, Pokémon and Mr. Pokémon, and Crowley's exile and return. The reward is BUFFTAIL.

A craft note from Claude. The mafia works best as cartoonish, incompetent criminals, with the joke aimed at the absurd chain of coincidences and at Crowley, and not at any real community.

## The adult humor and its limits

The audience is true genwunners aged thirty and over, and the crude sexual humor is part of the design and not a side effect. Its subject is non-monogamous and sexual liberation practices, including NTR and hotwifing, together with clothing norms and monogamy norms. The cast includes people who feel liberated from those norms and also people who argue against them. The author says these are commonly joked about adult tropes. The link to the lore is that restrictive boundaries are treated as an Ahrimanic influence, so the humor and the metaphysics push the same theme.

Claude's suggestion for where each side lives in the world follows the areas. Area 1's priests and policemen preach one thing and do another, which gives the hypocrisy jokes. Area 2's commune has no body shame, so it is the liberated pole. Area 3's state files love in triplicate and punishes the wrong bonds, so it is the restricted pole and the target of the ledger jokes. Area 4 sells forgetting, so a clinic that removes jealousy and guilt for a monthly fee fits it exactly. Trainer classes can carry the debate, for example a naturist, a swinging couple, a monogamy evangelist and a relationship auditor with a spreadsheet.

The debate should be fair. Liberated characters are not saints, critics are not strawmen, and each side gets one good argument and one ridiculous flaw. The jokes aim at hypocrisy, jealousy, bureaucracy and bad ideas, and not at people for who they are.

The form is text only. The comedy uses innuendo, double meanings, wordplay and awkward situations, with no explicit description. These limits are fixed. All people involved are adults and the jokes assume consent. No sprite is nude, so bathers wear towels or swimwear. No child appears in or near any such scene. The Amphitheater ritual is faded out and never drawn. The title screen should carry an adults-only warning.

## Audio, art and credits

New Bark Town and Cherrygrove keep vanilla Crystal music. Everywhere else the game uses a long, silent, Lavender Town style ambient loop that does not get boring, in keeping with the sleeping aid joke. The author accepts using far fewer music files than the original, partly to fit 2 MB, so the ROM keeps the vanilla songs for the first two towns and cuts most of the rest. Battle music is still an open question.

All character sprites are original, generated in code, and none depict religious figures. About fifty overworld character sprites were made this way. The map look mixes vanilla Crystal tilesets with new looks inspired by the author's sheets, so different lanes feel like different worlds. Drawing a full set of custom tilesets was tried and shelved in favor of vanilla ones, because the mix of existing tilesets already gives the colliding-worlds look and costs far less. Each outdoor map has a hard limit of about 18 walking sprites plus the item ball, or the player sprite garbles from running out of video memory.

Every new species needs its own front sprite, back sprite and cry. The Cthuludon cry rule from section 7 is the model, which is the first three syllables of the name as short notes.

The game credits Claude as co-creator, on the title screen or in the credits.

## Technical blueprint

The project is clean source edits on the public pret/pokecrystal disassembly at commit 3bc8daa, built with RGBDS 1.0.4 for the Rev 1 target (make crystal11), which produces a 2 MB MBC3 ROM. RGBDS 1.0.4 has to be built from source with CMake. Testing uses the PyBoy emulator driven from Python in headless mode.

The working method that scaled was to write deterministic Python generators for everything bulky, namely maps, trainers and their parties, the roster, moves, text pools and sprites, and to commit their output so the tree always builds from plain assembly. The generators reset from the base commit before running, so they can be re-run safely. A good pipeline order after any rule change is moves, then roster, then tips, then areas.

The main engine facts a builder needs are below.

| Engine fact | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Species IDs | One byte, so the roster tops out at 251 |
| Move categories | Each move needs a category byte and the damage code must read it |
| Type matchup table | Ends with a marker row, new type rows go before it, and the type count also drives the Pokédex search lists |
| Trainer parties | Stored per trainer class in separate ROM banks and copied into a 40 byte buffer, because one bank overflowed |
| Event flags | 256 to 1455 are used per trainer, and generated flags start at 1456 |
| Outdoor sprites | Maps load a fixed sprite list per map group, not the sprites on the map |
| Respawn after whiteout | Uses generated spawn points, and the table needs a row for every plaza |

Tests came from a test build with a _TESTMODE define. It skips the intro, spawns on any map with a ready party and optional items, and it never goes into the real ROM. The emulator scripts hook labels from the .sym file, trace the script command loop, take screenshot sheets, and walk whole lanes automatically to count kills. The type chart was verified by reading it back out of the built ROM and comparing it with the expected table.

| Pitfall | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text freezes the game | A custom text_asm block overwrote bc, the text cursor | Push and pop bc and return hl at the next text |
| Text runs into code bytes | A string before text_asm or text_ram lacked an at sign terminator | Always end such strings with at sign |
| Bare para, line or cont bytes misbehave | They are only valid inside a string | Start the block with text_start |
| Corpses show as standing people | The outdoor sprite list lacked the corpse sprite and the live object was stale | Add the sprite in AddOutdoorSprites, then delete and recopy the object struct |
| Player sprite garbles after warps | More than 18 walking sprites exceeded video memory | Cap the list and assert it in the generator |
| Pokémon have no moves | The learnset data sat in a different bank than its readers | Move the reading code, not the data |
| Whiteout always sends the player home | The spawn lookup stopped at the first empty row | Fix the lookup and add a spawn in each area |
| The test emulator seems to crash in battle | Reading WRAM bank 1 while the game had another bank mapped returned garbage | Read memory with the bank given in the symbol file |

## Suggested build order, backups and open questions

Build in small stages and back up after every one. The order below puts the rules and checks first and the content last, so a failure never costs more than one stage.

| Stage | Work | Proof it is done |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Base disassembly, stage 1 patch, test build with a skipped intro | The ROM builds and a test build spawns on any map |
| 2 | Type chart with Fairy, move categories, moves from Showdown data | The chart read back from the ROM matches the expected table |
| 3 | First kill reveal, kill counters, trainer tips | The reveal fires once and the counter rises per kill |
| 4 | Roster of four-stage lines with evolutions at 25, 50 and 75, Pokédex order | Every line evolves at the right levels and every base form is listed |
| 5 | One full area from a generator, with forced fights, a hub plaza and respawn | An automatic walker finishes the area and a whiteout respawns in it |
| 6 | The other areas from the same generator | The same walker passes each area |
| 7 | Milestones, rival crimelord, mansion bedroom ending, Area 5 | Both kill thresholds fire and the ending saves in the bedroom |
| 8 | Purgatorio, Nick's Landing, Steiner, Sabbah, Cioran, Crowley quest, Corkboard, true ending | Both endings are reachable |
| 9 | Wild encounters with every base form catchable, key item placement, quality of life changes, balance | A checker proves every base form can be caught |
| 10 | Music loops, new sprites and cries, Pokédex text, credits, adults-only warning | Hand playtest |
| 11 | The final ROM | Only after the author's last change list and approval |

For backups, keep the work in a private repository that is not the public pret one, and also send the author a patch file or a git bundle after each stage. The loss of this project came from keeping the only copy in a temporary workspace.

Open questions for the next builder to settle with the author include the following. Confirm the Normal and Bug immunity wording in section 6. Decide the exact form of BUFFTAIL. Place the last two key items. Choose battle music. Confirm the Cioran placement and the pact and ledger mechanic in section 10. Decide whether the game ever needs the MBC5 mapper for more than 2 MB. Also unresolved from the early roadmap are breeding, trading, day and night, and how evolution levels are read.
